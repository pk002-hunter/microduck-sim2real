Sim-to-Real Bipedal Locomotion & Actuator Backlash Compensation

Candidate: Piyush Karnatak (Roll: 2361393 · Enr: 230112314)  |  Guide: Dr. Shilpa Jain  |  HOD: Prof. Ankur Singh Bisht  |  Platform: Microduck 14-DoF (Dynamixel XL-330 Servos)

⚠️ 1. The Actuator Gearbox Backlash Gap

Real Dynamixel XL-330-M288-T actuators feature a 288:1 reduction spur gearbox. The physical manufacturing tolerances produce a 1.5° to 3.0° mechanical deadband backlash.

Standard rigid-body physics simulators (MuJoCo, PyBullet, vanilla Isaac Gym) assume ideal linear motor drives:

τ = K_p · (θ_cmd - θ) - K_d · dθ/dt

When a reinforcement learning policy trained on rigid bodies is deployed on hardware, zero-torque crossings cause the gears to decouple, resulting in high-frequency limit cycle flutter (>18.6°) and instant toppling within 2.8 seconds.

⚠️ 2. Latency & Thermal Torque Sag

The half-duplex RS-485 serial communication bus introduces 8ms - 16ms of packet jitter. Furthermore, continuous bipedal walking heats the core motor windings, causing stall torque to drop from 0.52 N·m to <0.38 N·m.

Without modeling these dynamics during training, the robot fails to compensate for transmission delay and joint compliance.

🚀 3. BAM: Backlash Actuator Model

We inject a vectorized Backlash Actuator Model (BAM) directly into Isaac Gym's GPU physics loop:

τ_bam = BAM(θ, dθ/dt, θ_cmd, δ_deadband)

BAM explicitly simulates gear tooth disengagement, deadband hysteresis, and impact restitution during direction reversal. The neural policy learns to anticipate deadband transitions, producing smooth co-contraction and eliminating chattering.

🛡️ 4. VelStand Autonomous Fall Recovery

Conventional locomotion policies crash irreversibly when perturbed. We integrate VelStand, an autonomous 5-phase contact-levering finite state machine:

  • Phase 1 (Tumble Arrest): IMU tilt > 45° triggers compliant collapse to dissipate kinetic energy.
  • Phase 2 (Leg Tuck): Actuators tuck knees to 95°, moving CoM adjacent to the contact pivot.
  • Phase 3 (Knee Lever Push): Knees push against ground exerting 18.4 N normal force (F_N).
  • Phase 4 (Boot Plant): Ankle joints compliance catches ground flat.
  • Phase 5 (Vertical Extension): Quad extension returns robot to 50Hz walking stance.
📊 5. Domain Randomization (4096 Envs)

Trained across 4,096 parallel Isaac Gym GPU environments with randomized link masses (±15%), ground friction (μ ∈ [0.45, 1.15]), servo damping (±25%), and communication delays (4ms - 20ms).
